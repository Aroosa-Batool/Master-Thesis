How should a reminder robot sense who is nearby?

Meet the robot and watch

Hypothetical AI-generated scene. Read the captions for sensing state, watch messages and delivery outcome.

Ohbot is a small desktop robot head connected to a computer. It delivers reminders scheduled beforehand, including sensitive appointments or payments. An external camera and a microphone can help the computer recognize whether another person is nearby. Ohbot’s eyes are not camera lenses.

Bangle.js displays private permission questions and reminder messages. A wrist display reduces public exposure, although someone looking at it could still read it. The watch connection is an approximate sign that the wearer is nearby, not proof of the exact room or that the watch is being worn. This reminder app does not use unrelated health or GPS data from the watch.

Three different decisions

Sensor access: permitting the microphone, camera, both or neither. This does not permit aloud disclosure.

Aloud disclosure: when another person is detected, the watch asks whether a sensitive reminder may be spoken. Yes permits speech; No withholds the content from speech and sends a private watch note.

Remembering: permitting a previous Yes or No to be stored and reused. The main stories ask each time and do not reuse an earlier answer.

The current watch app handles disclosure questions and private notes. Sensor and memory settings are configured on the computer; putting these setup choices on the watch is a proposed extension. Assume the sensing setup in each story was explicitly allowed before the scene.

The sensing approaches

Microphone only

The system listens for non-owner voices. With the unified app’s defaults it records continuously for five minutes, from seven to two minutes before a sensitive reminder. A silent visitor, or someone arriving after listening ends, may be missed. The camera is not used.

Camera only

In the current camera-only application the camera stays open during the running session, with a head scan used for reminder checks. It may miss a face outside its view, hidden from it or incorrectly recognized. The microphone is not used for presence sensing.

Microphone first, camera fallback

The microphone listens first. When a non-owner voice is detected, the camera is not activated. Otherwise, the microphone closes and the camera scans. With the unified app’s defaults this can continue for about two minutes until just before delivery. Both sensors are permitted but never active simultaneously.

These approaches have different activation durations. Detection can be wrong. If no visitor is detected, the system can speak a sensitive reminder without a disclosure question. A No or no reply withholds sensitive speech and attempts watch delivery. If the watch is disconnected, that private delivery is not guaranteed. All stories assume a working watch connection.

What is stored?

The presence pipeline processes audio and frames on the computer. Raw presence recordings and frames are temporary during ordinary reminder delivery. Numerical face and voice recognition representations are saved locally to recognize owners and visitors later. These are identifying data, even though they are not replayable recordings.

Even the repeated-question mode can retain recognition representations. Reminder text and timing are saved. Remembering mode also saves explicit disclosure answers. Logs can include reminder content, person IDs and decisions. The code does not establish an automatic deletion period for all these files. A separate evaluation-data mode intentionally saves raw images or voice clips; it is outside these ordinary reminder stories.

Story 1 of 6 · Microphone only
Appointment

Hypothetical AI-generated scene. Read the captions for sensing state, watch messages and delivery outcome.

      LocationAlex’s living roomPeopleAlex and Sam, a close friend visiting for tea
      
Context: Alex scheduled a sensitive reminder: “your mental-health appointment at 3 PM today”. Sam remains nearby throughout sensing and delivery.

Previously allowed sensing: Microphone only. Disclosure policy: ask each time a visitor is detected; do not remember disclosure answers.

1. Conversation and sensing

During the pre-reminder interval, Sam says, “Would you like some tea?” Alex replies, “Yes, thanks.” Sam’s face is visible to the robot if the camera is used.

The microphone listens during the five-minute window before the reminder. Sam’s voice is detected. The camera is not used.

2. Private watch question

Robot asks: “I have noticed that someone is present with you. Do you want me to send private reminders in front of them?”

Watch buttons: Yes / No. Here, “send … in front of them” means speaking aloud.

3. Alex’s response and delivery

Alex taps No.

Ohbot says only “Hello there.” The connected watch displays “Reminder: your mental-health appointment at 3 PM today” as a private note. Sam does not hear the reminder content.

Should disclosure decisions be remembered?

This is a separate preference. Ask each time means asking again when a visitor is detected for a sensitive reminder. Remember means reusing an explicit Yes or No when that visitor is recognized.

Face and voice identities are separate, so the same person may prompt again through a different sensor. Recognition can be wrong. These are the wearer’s disclosure decisions, not permission from the visitor.
